## The IDR in one glance
- **Scale:** Limited → Emerging → Developing → Proficient → Advanced. Advanced means "adapts it across situations and often strengthens others through it."
- **Evidence:** what someone would actually see, hear or experience from you.
- **Leadership competencies:** Self-Awareness · Adaptability · Collaboration · Emotional Intelligence · Feedback (giving + receiving) · Initiative.
- **Business competencies:** Written Communication · Verbal Communication · Professional Presence · Time Management · Project Management · Problem Solving.
- **Closing reflection:** "If someone were drafting a professional team three months from now, what competency do you want them to choose you for?"

## How a trial works
1. **System Briefing.** The host reveals the round's trial and attribute, as Prompt 3 already does. Each team gets one question card.
2. **Think time, 30 seconds of silence.** This helps reflective and introverted players (Intrapersonal). Without it, the fastest talkers would always answer first.
3. **Answer, 90 seconds.** One player is the **Lead** and answers. Each teammate may add one line. The Lead rotates every round, so over 6 rounds everyone on a team of 4 leads at least once.
4. **Score with the Proficient Card.** Win = the answer hits 3 of 4 checks. Otherwise it's a Loss. The host enters the result as usual.

### Proficient Card: the 4 checks
Built from the IDR's "evidence," "what would this look like," and "what would this sound like" prompts.
1. **Answers the real question** (Problem Solving: "focus on the right problem").
2. **Uses a real example from the cycle,** something someone would have seen or heard (IDR evidence standard).
3. **Explains the reasoning:** "Here's how I got to this conclusion…"
4. **Includes a quieter voice:** at least one teammate besides the Lead adds something (Collaboration: "include voices that may be quiet or overlooked").

Why this helps the game: Win/Loss currently depends on host judgment alone. The card makes it fair, quick and the same for every team. It also teaches the IDR's standard of evidence while people play.

## Questions by round
The rounds and attributes come from Base44 Prompt 2. Each round has 4 cards, one per team, so teams can't copy each other. Wording is generalized: no card names a person, and none reveals anyone's dossier or scores.

### Round 1: Boot Up · Initiative (cost 2)
Cycle moment: Team Week, Know Your Lens, teams formed, the SOP challenge. IDR: Initiative, Self-Awareness, Professional Presence.
1. Tell us about a time in the first two weeks when you moved something forward before anyone asked.
2. What do you naturally pay more attention to on a team: getting the work organized, keeping people connected, or both? Give an example. *(from a dossier ASK question)*
3. First impressions: what did you want your cohort to understand about you in week one, and what did you actually do to show it?
4. During the SOP challenge, what step did your team miss at first, and who noticed it?

**Why these questions:** Boot Up is about stepping in. These cards reward naming a real action, not a personality type.
**How it helps the game:** an easy, low-cost start. Everyone practices the evidence habit before the costs go up.

### Round 2: Sprint 1 · Focus (cost 3)
Cycle moment: team agreement, the first Medline meeting, backlog in Plaky, back-planning, prioritizing under pressure. IDR: Time Management, Project Management, Written Communication.
1. You have three tasks due tomorrow and time for two. Walk us through how you decide.
2. When a teammate misses a deadline, what is your first instinct: understand what happened, reset the plan, or something else? *(dossier ASK)*
3. Describe a status update you wrote during Sprint 1. What made it clear, or what would you change?
4. What team tools or routines help you make sure good conversations turn into completed work? *(dossier ASK)*

**Why these questions:** Time Management and Project Management were common growth areas on the IDR. It also stretches the task side of teamwork.
**How it helps the game:** Focus shows up twice (Rounds 2 and 4). This round teaches teams to plan before the bigger Focus cost in Round 4.

### Round 3: The Mirror · Composure (cost 3) · **cross-team round**
Cycle moment: the Medline presentation. It was praised for confidence and attitude in Q&A, with feedback about reading from the tablet and an unclear Incident vs. Problem split. IDR: Feedback, Emotional Intelligence, Verbal Communication, Professional Presence.
**Mirror twist:** teams interview each other. Team A asks Team B its card and scores B with the Proficient Card, while B scores A, and so on. The host confirms the results.
1. Tell us about feedback that was hard to hear. What did you do with it in the next week?
2. Give one piece of honest, respectful feedback to the team in front of you about how they just answered.
3. When you're under pressure, what would your teammates notice first, and what helps you reset?
4. Once you have a strong recommendation, what helps you stay open to a teammate who sees the problem differently? *(dossier ASK)*

**Why these questions:** The Mirror is the cycle's feedback moment, so the round turns feedback into the mechanic itself. Players give and receive it live, which is the IDR's Feedback competency in action.
**How it helps the game:** teams from different groups get to know each other, which helps players make better guesses in the Glitch Report accusations. It also fits Composure: staying steady while being evaluated.

### Round 4: Geek Week · Focus (cost 4) · **hands-on round**
Cycle moment: testing, catching up on artifacts, LW6A Adaptability, BW6B Organize for Outcomes. IDR: Problem Solving, Project Management.
**Hands-on twist (kinesthetic):** each team gets 6 sticky notes of tasks to sort by priority in 30 seconds. Then the Lead explains the order using the card's question.
1. Sort these tasks. What did you put first, and what did you choose to leave undone?
2. Something broke in testing an hour before it's due. What do you check first?
3. When you have enough information to act but people still disagree, how do you decide whether to keep discussing or move forward? *(dossier ASK)*
4. What helps you move from reflection to a decision when the team cannot wait for perfect clarity? *(dossier ASK)*

**Why these questions:** Geek Week was testing and catch-up, so these cards are practical "what do you do first" questions. They give an edge to grounded, concrete thinkers.
**How it helps the game:** it covers the third learning style. Rounds 1–3 were talk and listening; this one is hands-on. It's also the most expensive Focus round, so a clear, physical task makes the outcome feel fair.

### Round 5: Sprint 2 · Collaboration (cost 4) · **joint panel**
Cycle moment: Resolution Plan (communication gaps, file-version chaos), Verbal Presence workshop, Team Inclusion Audit. IDR: Collaboration, Verbal Communication.
**Joint panel twist:** pair the teams (A+B, C+D). Each pair answers one card together as an 8-person panel and wins or loses together. This changes the Proficient Card's "quieter voice" check to need 2 non-Lead voices.
1. Your team had file-version chaos. As a joint team, agree on one rule that would have fixed it, and explain why.
2. Tell us about a time you supported a teammate without taking over.
3. What do you do when supporting a teammate and protecting the team's standard pull in different directions? *(dossier ASK)*
4. How do you make sure the team has heard enough perspectives without slowing the decision more than necessary? *(dossier ASK)*

**Why these questions:** Collaboration and communication gaps were real Sprint 2 issues in the timeline. A joint panel makes players collaborate with people outside their own team, under time pressure.
**How it helps the game:** the team structure stays the same, but for one round players depend on another team. That raises the stakes for gifts and sabotage in that round's Open Floor.

### Round 6: Sprint 3 · Grit (cost 6)
Cycle moment: finishing, polishing, testing, handoff, Dream Speeches. IDR: Adaptability, Initiative, Problem Solving (not giving up).
1. Tell us about a time you were stuck on a problem and didn't give up. What got you unstuck?
2. Your plan changes the week before the deadline. Walk us through your first hour.
3. When the team has to make a fast decision that isn't your preferred option, what helps you commit and move forward? *(dossier ASK)*
4. What is one thing you're doing now that you couldn't do in week one?

**Why these questions:** Grit is the highest-cost round, so the questions are about persistence and growth across the whole cycle. Card 4 makes players look back over all 14 weeks.
**How it helps the game:** it's the emotional peak before Graduation. Players who glitched earlier can still win the trial by telling a strong story.

### Round 7: Graduation (no cost)
Everyone answers the IDR closing reflection, one sentence each, to the whole room: **"If someone were drafting a professional team three months from now, what competency would you want them to choose you for?"**
**How it helps the game:** it's not scored. It's a shared ending that closes on growth rather than points, and it suits the "real world" screen for graduates.

## Ghost curveballs (optional)
The three ghosts (USER_???, ATTEMPT_01, NULL) can whisper one curveball follow-up per game. The host uses it when a team's answer is too general. The ghost cards draw on the "missing record" theme only and never refer to real people.
- **USER_???:** "You said 'we.' What did *you* do?"
- **ATTEMPT_01:** "That didn't work the first time. What did you change on the second try?"
- **NULL:** "Someone on the team goes quiet mid-project. What do you notice, and what do you do?"

## Learning styles covered
- **Visual:** the question shows on every screen.
- **Auditory:** the Lead or interviewer reads it out loud.
- **Kinesthetic:** the sticky-note sort in Round 4.
- **Intrapersonal:** 30-second silent think time every round.
- **Interpersonal:** cross-team rounds 3 and 5.

## Team setup recommendations
- **One game room, not separate servers.** Use one join code with 16 players in 4 teams of 4, plus a host who doesn't play. The core rules depend on teams sharing a room: accusations must target someone on another team, sabotage and gifts cross teams, and the Auditor and Lifeline paths need other teams.
- **The 3 hidden types are enough for 16.** A type is a secret role, not a group, so many players share each one. To protect the 71% graduation tuning, suggest a soft cap of about 4 Contrarians (roughly 1 per team). If more pick Contrarian, re-test.
- **More variety without more types:** each player's office tool (Coffee Mug, Sticky Note, and so on) could be chosen from the IDR competency they say they bring strongly. That personalizes the game without changing the economy.
- **Host:** for all interns to play, a staff member or mentor should host.
